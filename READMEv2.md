# The Billion-Dollar Myth
**A behavioural, incentives-first analysis of how “success” in film and television shifted from box-office dominance to ecosystem leverage.**

## What this is
This project began with a widely held assumption: **cinema box office equals success**.

**Act I** examines long-horizon behavioural patterns that challenge this assumption, including:
- gradual audience behaviour change (the “frog in the pot” effect), and
- the *decay of meaning* in milestone metrics, where once-rare signals (e.g. $1B box office) lose explanatory power through repetition.

**Act II** reframes the industry through **consumer behaviour, ritual formation, cultural gravity, and ecosystem economics**, rather than deal-specific speculation or short-term headlines.

**This is a case-study-style analysis, not a prediction.**  
It relies on public information, cautious inference, and explicit acknowledgement of uncertainty. No certainty theatre.

## Method (high-level)
- Prioritise **revealed behaviour** over stated intent.
- Separate **measurement** (what is counted) from **meaning** (what it actually reflects).
- Distinguish **traits** (stable incentives and structures) from **states** (temporary conditions or shocks).
- Explicitly label inference strength:
  - **Strong** — supported by multi-year behavioural patterns
  - **Speculative** — plausible but not confirmable with current public data
